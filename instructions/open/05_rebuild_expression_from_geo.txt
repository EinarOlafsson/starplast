REBUILD THE EXISTING EXPRESSION COLUMNS FROM THE GEO SOURCES -- NEEDS A DECISION
================================================================================

STATE
    The GEO source matrices are downloaded and sitting in
    datasets/transcription/RNAseq/: GSE108740_FPKM.xlsx and both GSE206344
    tables.

    The node table does NOT use them. Its rna108740_* and rna206344_* columns
    come from the upstream toxonet build, already processed by someone else.
    The six datasets added in expression.py DO go through sources.normalise().

    So the table now mixes two provenances: 18 columns processed upstream by
    unknown means, and 70 processed here by a declared rule.

WHY IT MATTERS
    Mostly consistency, and it is not urgent. But the evidence that it matters
    is concrete: the upstream rna108740_* columns top out at 9.7 while the GEO
    source reaches 16,520, so a transform was applied that nobody here can
    name. Anything that treats those 18 columns as comparable with the 70 is
    assuming something unverified.

WHAT TO DO
    Only with the user's agreement -- it changes every existing expression
    column and therefore the embedding, the clusters and every published
    figure derived from them.

    If yes: load both GEO matrices through sources.normalise("fpkm") and
    ("log_intensity") respectively, replace the rna* columns, rebuild, and
    diff the embedding before and after. Report how far genes moved.

HOW TO KNOW IT WORKED
    Every expression column has one declared quantification type and one
    declared transform. sources.infer_quant() agrees with the declaration.

TRAPS
    Do not "harmonise" by logging the upstream columns again. They are already
    logged. Range decides, not filename -- see 00_INDEX.


================================================================================
DECIDED 2026-08-11 -- "do whatever is most robust"
================================================================================

The question was put as GEO-primary OR supplement-derived. Answered as: neither
alone is the robust option, because each fails in a way the other detects.

    supplement-derived   what ships today. Static files, exactly what the
                         authors published, but not re-derivable by a reader
                         and not checkable against anything.
    GEO-primary          re-downloadable and automatable end to end, but a
                         reprocessed series can change under you silently, and
                         GEO's matrix is not always the one the paper used.

So: BUILD THE GEO PATH, AND USE IT AS A CHECK RATHER THAN A REPLACEMENT.

    - fetch the GEO processed matrix for every series that has one
    - join to the shipped columns on canonical accession, via identity.py
    - report per column: n genes in common, Spearman rho, and any gene whose
      rank differs by more than a stated margin
    - where they AGREE, prefer GEO as the shipped source: it is re-derivable
      by a reader, which is the property the paper needs
    - where they DISAGREE, do not quietly pick one. Investigate, record the
      reason in the registry, and keep the supplement value with a note

    Pin both with checksums so a changed remote is caught rather than absorbed.

This is more work than either single option and is the only version that can
detect its own failure, which is what "most robust" has to mean for a project
whose every historical bug was silent.

The comparison itself is a result worth reporting in the paper: it is direct
evidence about whether published supplements match their own deposits.
