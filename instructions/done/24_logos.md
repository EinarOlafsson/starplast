# Forty logo drafts, black and white — DONE 2026-08-12

`starplast/data/icons/logo_01_*.svg` … `logo_40_*.svg`, generated by `scripts/generate_logos.py`.

## Generated rather than drawn, for three reasons

Forty hand-drawn variations would be forty variations on whichever one came first. A script makes
the six directions actually distinct; the seeds mean "that one, but sparser" can be answered by
changing a number rather than by redrawing something nobody can reproduce; and it lets the 16-pixel
constraint be a **test** rather than a hope.

## Seven directions, forty-nine drafts

    constellation (8)  the map IS a point cloud, with nearest-neighbour edges rather than random
                       ones — a constellation whose lines cross at random reads as a scribble
    apicoplast (6)     nested ovals for the plastid the name refers to; no nucleus, no apex, nothing
                       that reads as a cell diagram
    crescent (6)       the tachyzoite, apex up, solid or made of points
    orbit (6)          concentric rings, for the three levels of detail
    letter S (7)       an S built from points, so it reads as a letter at a glance and as data close
    dark field (7)     the project's argument: most of this proteome has never been studied. Most of
                       the mark is faint rings and a few points are lit. The inverted drafts say the
                       same thing the other way round — a black field with the studied genes knocked
                       out of it.
    galaxy (8)         asked for after the first sheet was looked at: a TILTED system with orbits and
                       *Toxoplasma* at the centre. Tilted rather than face-on because a face-on
                       system is a target and a tilted one is a system seen from somewhere, which is
                       what a 3D map you can orbit around is. The planets sit on their orbit's own
                       parametric points rather than being scattered near it — scattered they read as
                       dirt rather than as bodies going round something. The orbits are knocked out
                       behind the parasite: without that the innermost one runs straight through it
                       and the middle of the mark is a blob rather than the body the system turns
                       around.

    galaxy_9           the last of the series, and the one that answers an objection to the other
                       eight: they stamp a crescent OVER the middle of the system, which is two
                       drawings in the same place. Here the innermost of three evenly spaced,
                       evenly weighted rings IS the parasite, so the system resolves into the
                       organism as it gets smaller instead of being interrupted by it. It is
                       apicomplexan by its SILHOUETTE — tapered to a point at one end, round at the
                       other, with the apical cap said in one short arc — rather than by drawn
                       organelles: at 16 pixels a rhoptry is three dark pixels and the difference
                       between a parasite and a bean is lost anyway, while the taper survives.

Forty was the ask; the galaxy family is a seventh direction requested after the first forty were
drawn, and adding it beat discarding drafts nobody had rejected. Forty-nine in total.

## The 16-pixel test did its job four times

Every draft is rendered at 16 pixels and its darkness measured, composited on white — the worst case
for a mark that also has to work on a light theme. Four whole families failed it on the first pass
and were redrawn heavier: the apicoplast at 3% ink (four hairlines are a grey smear), the crescent
whose 1.6-unit outline is a third of a pixel at that size, the orbit rings at 2%, and the dark-field
drafts whose 0.6-unit rings vanished entirely — which turned "most of the field is unstudied" into
"most of the field is absent", a different and wrong claim.

The measure is darkness, not coverage: the inverted drafts are a solid tile with points knocked out
of it, so by coverage they are 100% "ink" and by darkness they are what they look like. Both bounds
guard the same failure — no contrast at the size it will actually be seen.

Also tested: no greys anywhere in the sources, all forty distinct, each carrying a `<title>` saying
what it argues, none containing anything from the cell artwork, and regenerating produces the same
forty byte for byte.

## A postscript from consolidating

Running the suite in the working checkout — the one where searches have actually been run — failed
`test_the_cache_size_is_quoted_correctly`: it walked the whole data directory and found 81 MB against
the documented 17. The documents were right. The 69 MB is saved embeddings, which are gitignored,
written by running the application, and the user's own work. The test now measures the shipped cache
only, skipping `embeddings/`, `runs/` and `logs/`. Left as it was, using the application would have
looked like the documentation drifting.
